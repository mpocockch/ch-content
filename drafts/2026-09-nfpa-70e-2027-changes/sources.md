# Sources — "What Is Changing in NFPA 70E's Next Edition?"

## Editorial note on the topic's original framing

The Approved Ideas card for this topic (gid `1217455522370190`, an agent-research candidate from
2026-08-13) asserted that "the NFPA 70E 2027 edition took effect May 6, 2026" and wrote the whole
piece around that premise. Verification before drafting found this date is very likely wrong:

- NFPA's own published development calendar for this cycle (per NETA World Journal's Nov. 2025
  column by Ron Widup, a longtime NFPA 70E Technical Committee figure, and EC&M's "Two NFPA
  Standards for Workplace Safety") puts the Second Draft report on/before March 2026, NITMAM
  Motions Committee review on May 12, 2026, the membership Technical Meeting June 25–26, 2026,
  and Standards Council issuance expected in the fall of 2026 — assuming no further delay.
- That calendar makes a finalized, enforceable edition on May 6, 2026 implausible: the standard
  was still mid-ballot at that point.
- The "May 6, 2026" date (and variants — one source found gives "April 16, 2026," another says
  "later in 2026" in its own body text while a search-engine annotation on the same page claims a
  different date) appears to be an artifact that has propagated across a cluster of
  similarly-styled SEO/content-mill pages, not something traceable to NFPA itself.
- **`nfpa.org` and all primary/top-tier trade-press domains (ecmag.com, ecmweb.com,
  netaworldjournal.org) are blocked by this environment's egress proxy** — confirmed by a direct
  `WebFetch` attempt against a specific nfpa.org URL, which returned `EGRESS_BLOCKED`. Every claim
  below is therefore sourced from `WebSearch` result snippets/synthesis, one step removed from
  primary text, not from a direct read of NFPA's own pages or the standard itself.

Given that, the article was reframed from "the 2027 edition is already in effect" to "the next
edition is in the final stages of NFPA's process, here's what's coming and how to prepare" — and
the false premise itself became a named Misconception section, since correcting a claim a
practitioner would recognize as wrong is more valuable (and safer) than repeating it. This
follows `DRAFTING.md`'s accuracy discipline directly: "If research shows a standard does not
contain something commonly assumed, say so plainly."

**A human with working access to nfpa.org / NFPA LiNK should confirm current issuance status
before this goes further** — "fall 2026" is now, so the edition may have been issued very
recently, or may still be pending. Nothing in this draft asserts either way; see the "Where
things stand as of publication" section in `draft.md`.

## Claims used in the body, with tier and confidence

All of the below are Tier 2/3 (trade press, recognized industry commentary) via `WebSearch`
synthesis only — no primary NFPA text was read. Multiple independent, differently-styled sources
converging on the same specifics raised confidence for some; single-source or content-mill-only
claims were either dropped or generalized.

- **Additional-person requirement for energized work** — Tier 2. Corroborated across
  independently-styled sources: an apparent NFPA blog post title
  (`nfpa.org/news-blogs-and-articles/blogs/2026/06/12/nfpa-70e-change-two-person-requirement-for-energized-work`,
  title only — content not directly read, blocked), NFPA Xchange community
  (`community.nfpa.org/news/11532318`), Tyndale
  (`tyndaleusa.com/nfpa-70e/130-2-a2-additional-person-requirement-for-energized-work`), and Jim
  Phillips/ECMag ("Is a Second Person Needed? New requirements coming in NFPA 70E 2027"). Used in
  the body without citing an exact section number, since that number (130.2(A)(2)) comes only from
  secondary sources I could not verify against primary text.
- **Contact-thermal PPE distinct from shock PPE** — Tier 3, general description used, no specific
  section number cited in the body (not confirmed against primary text).
- **Absence-of-voltage testing exception for current-transformer secondaries / series circuits**
  — Tier 2/3 (Brainfiller, `brainfiller.com/technical-articles/absence-of-current-nfpa-70e`, and
  general Article 120 discussion). Used without citing a specific exception number.
- **New DC-hazard coverage (batteries, capacitors, photovoltaic/solar)** — weakest-sourced claim.
  Specific article numbers (310/360/370/380) appeared only in lower-tier SEO sources and were not
  cross-confirmed by higher-tier sources, so **the body deliberately omits article numbers** and
  describes the change only in general terms (new DC-hazard coverage), per "if a number cannot be
  verified, leave it out of the body."
- **Articles 90, 105, 110, 120 and 130 under revision** — Tier 2. Jim Phillips/ECMag two-part
  series titles: "2027 NFPA 70E Update: A look at the significant changes to articles 90, 105, 110
  and 120" and "Changes to Article 130: 2027 NFPA 70E update, part 2." Titles only (article bodies
  not directly readable, domain blocked); used as confirmation that these are the articles seeing
  substantive work, not for specific content within each.
- **Condition-of-maintenance / NFPA 70B connection** — the sourced version of this claim was a
  two-hop chain (a new informational note under 110.3(C) pointing to Informative Annex S, which in
  turn references NFPA 70B) found only on a vendor content site (graceport.com), which is the
  weakest tier of source found and part of the same content cluster flagged above as unreliable on
  dates. **The body deliberately does not cite the 110.3(C)/Annex S section numbers** and instead
  describes the connection in general terms only.
- **NFPA 70E cycle (2018 → 2021 → 2024 → next edition on a 3-year cycle)** — general knowledge,
  consistent with every source checked; not disputed anywhere.
- **NFPA 70B / OSHA General Duty Clause relationship** — general knowledge already used in the
  published "Breaker Testing" exemplar post on this same blog; not re-verified here beyond
  confirming no source contradicts it.
