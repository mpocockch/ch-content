After an electrical incident, the first questions are about the equipment.

When was it last maintained? What do the records show?

Three documents shape the answers, and most facilities treat them as three separate projects. They are one question seen from three sides.

→ NFPA 70B covers how the equipment is maintained
→ NFPA 70E covers how people work on or near it
→ The OSHA General Duty Clause lets an inspector hold an employer to both as recognized good practice
→ Thin maintenance records weaken the assumptions behind your arc flash labels

NFPA 70E already ties safe work to the condition of maintenance. The next edition points readers to guidance that references NFPA 70B, so your safety standard now sends you to your maintenance standard.

OSHA does not enforce 70E or 70B directly. It does use them as evidence of what a reasonable employer should have been doing.
