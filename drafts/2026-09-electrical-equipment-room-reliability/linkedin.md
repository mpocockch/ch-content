Your electrical room is probably storing something it shouldn't.

Boxes next to the panel. A ladder against the switchgear. Files stacked in front of a breaker
someone may need to reach in a hurry.

NEC 110.26 requires clear working space in front of electrical equipment — a minimum of 3 feet
at low voltage, growing at higher voltages and where live parts are exposed on both sides. A
locked door doesn't satisfy it. Storage creep inside that clearance is one of the most common
findings during electrical inspections, in rooms that are otherwise well run.

Clearance is only one layer. What tends to turn up in rooms nobody has assessed as a system:
→ Ambient heat quietly aging insulation across every piece of gear at once
→ Humidity swings producing condensation that tracks across a bus
→ Panel schedules that stopped matching reality years ago
→ No environmental monitoring on rooms feeding life-safety or production loads

A room can have every protective device individually tested and passing and still be
unreliable. Reliability is a property of the room, not of one test result.

We put together a guide covering what NEC 110.26 and NFPA 70B actually require, how the bar
shifts across commercial, manufacturing, healthcare and data center facilities, and how to
build a program around the room itself.
