Most facilities treat panel schedules as paperwork.

The NEC doesn't.

408.4(A) requires every circuit to be identified by its clear, evident, and specific purpose.
Not "lights." Not a tenant name that moves out next year — the code specifically bars
descriptions that depend on who currently occupies a space.

Where facilities get caught:
→ Directories that haven't been touched since the last fit-out
→ Circuits labeled by department instead of function
→ "Spare" on a breaker that's actually live
→ A current arc flash label sitting on a panel whose directory is years out of date

That last one matters more than it sounds. The arc flash label under NFPA 70E 130.5(H) and the
circuit directory under 408.4(A) are separate requirements. Having one current says nothing
about the other.

And the real cost isn't the citation. It's the technician who locks out the wrong breaker
during lockout/tagout because the directory told them to. It's the responder killing power
during an emergency from a directory that lies.

We broke down what 408.4(A) actually requires, why an inaccurate directory is a safety problem
and not only a compliance one, and what it costs to let one drift.
