Your generator ran every month this year. Can you prove it passed?

In a hospital, the test is only half the job. The other half is a record a surveyor can follow.

NFPA 99 and the standards it points to set specific, named intervals. Most facilities do the work. Fewer can show it.

What a surveyor expects to find:
→ A monthly generator run of at least 30 minutes, at 30% of nameplate or the manufacturer's exhaust temperature
→ Every transfer switch on the essential electrical system cycled monthly
→ A continuous 4-hour generator run every 36 months
→ Line isolation monitor tests with a result, not just a checkmark
→ Receptacle intervals backed by documented performance data

And the edition matters. CMS still surveys hospitals to the 2012 edition of NFPA 99, while the 2021 edition added a maintenance program, built on NFPA 70B, for everything serving Category 1 and 2 spaces.

We put together a guide to the NFPA 99 electrical testing every hospital facility manager should have documented.
