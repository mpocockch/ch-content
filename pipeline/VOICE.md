# Voice and banned phrases

C&H Electric writes for people who maintain electrical systems for a living: plant engineers,
facility directors, hospital and manufacturing maintenance leads in Connecticut. They are
technical, busy, and sceptical of marketing language.

## Tone

- Direct and concrete. Lead with the finding, not the context.
- Specific over impressive: "a 480V switchboard with no arc flash labels" beats "aging
  electrical infrastructure."
- Confident about what we know, explicit about what we don't.
- Never talk down. Assume the reader knows what a breaker is.
- Second person is the default — "your facility", "if you're unsure".
- **First-person plural is allowed where it carries field experience**, which is how the
  published posts use it: "Our technicians at C&H Electric are certified to NETA QEMC
  standards." What is banned is corporate we-speak that asserts rather than informs — "we are
  committed to excellence", "we pride ourselves on". Two or three such mentions per post.

## Banned phrases

Do not use these. The list is not exhaustive — it is a sample of the register to avoid.

- dive into / deep dive
- game-changer
- navigate the landscape / navigate the complexities
- leverage *(as a verb)*
- delve
- crucial
- elevate
- robust
- unlock
- in today's fast-paced world
- it's important to note that
- when it comes to
- seamless / seamlessly
- empower
- cutting-edge
- landscape *(figurative)*

Also avoid: opening a paragraph with "Additionally" or "Furthermore"; hedging stacks
("may potentially be able to"); and headings phrased as questions the section then restates
instead of answering.

## Formatting

- Sentence case for headings.
- Numbers as digits from 10 up; spell out below, except with units (`4 hours`, `480V`).
- Standards on first mention in full, then short: `NFPA 70E` → `70E`.
- No em-dash pileups; one per sentence at most.
